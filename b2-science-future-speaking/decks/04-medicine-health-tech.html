Medicine & Health Technology

Practice Part 1 interview, Part 2 photo comparison, Part 3 collaborative task, and extra homework.

Part 1 — Interview

Part 1 — Interview

Answer these questions. Spend about 20 seconds on each.

1. Do you ever use technology to monitor your health, such as a fitness watch or an app?
Yes, I wear a fitness tracker that records my steps and heart rate. It motivates me to stay active, although I occasionally find the constant notifications rather intrusive.
2. To what extent do you trust online medical information?
I find reliable sites genuinely helpful for minor concerns, but I never rely on them exclusively. A qualified doctor's diagnosis is unquestionably more trustworthy.
3. How might telemedicine transform healthcare in rural areas?
Telemedicine could dramatically improve access, allowing patients in remote regions to consult specialists without travelling considerable distances.
4. Are you concerned about the privacy of your medical data?
I am increasingly concerned, especially when apps share information with third parties. Stronger regulation would undoubtedly make people feel considerably safer.
5. Would you be willing to have a medical check-up from a robot or an AI system?
I would be cautiously open to it for routine checks, since AI can analyse results remarkably quickly. However, I would still prefer a human doctor for serious news.
Useful phrases:
As far as I'm concerned...I'm inclined to think...It stands to reason that...Having said that...I'm somewhat sceptical about...On the whole...
Part 2 — Long Turn

Part 2 — Photo Comparison

Talk for 1 minute. Compare the photos and answer the question.

Why do you think people are increasingly using technology to manage their health?
In the first photo, a person is checking their heart rate on a smartwatch, which displays a reading of 104 beats per minute. In the second photo, a doctor in a white coat is having an online video consultation with a patient in his surgery. I think people increasingly rely on health technology because it makes monitoring effortless and considerably more affordable. Wearable devices provide remarkably instant feedback, while telemedicine removes the need to travel and wait in crowded clinics. Admittedly, these tools cannot replace a thorough examination, yet they undeniably encourage people to take greater responsibility for their wellbeing.
Part 3 — Discussion

Part 3 — Collaborative Task

Discuss with your partner for 2–3 minutes, then decide.

A hospital has received funding for new medical technology. Discuss the ideas and decide which two would be most beneficial for patients.
  • Robot systems to assist in surgery
  • Wearable devices that monitor patients at home
  • Online appointments with specialists
  • AI software to analyse X-rays faster
  • 3D printers to make custom prosthetics
A: I believe AI software for analysing X-rays would be exceptionally valuable, since it could detect problems remarkably early.
B: That's a compelling point. Wearable monitors also appeal to me, because they let patients recover comfortably at home.
A: True, although the devices would have to be genuinely reliable and simple to use.
B: I agree. Online appointments are convenient, but perhaps less groundbreaking than the other options.
A: Fair enough. So shall we choose the AI software and the wearable devices?
B: Absolutely, I'm entirely in agreement with you.
Part 4 — Extension

Part 4 — Follow-up Discussion

Answer these deeper questions.

1. Should everyone be entitled to free healthcare, regardless of income?
I strongly believe healthcare should be universally accessible, because illness is never a choice. A society that protects its most vulnerable is unquestionably fairer.
2. What are the risks of relying too heavily on medical technology?
Over-reliance could make doctors less attentive, and technical failures might delay treatment. Privacy is another considerable concern.
3. How could genetics change medicine in the coming decades?
Genetic testing may allow treatments tailored to an individual's DNA, potentially preventing diseases before symptoms appear.
4. Is it right to use technology to help people live much longer?
Extending healthy life is undoubtedly desirable, yet it raises profound questions about resources and overpopulation that society must address.
Vocabulary

Vocabulary & Phrases

telemedicine — medical care given remotely, usually by video call or phone

wearable — a small electronic device you wear, such as a watch that tracks your health

diagnosis — the identification of an illness or problem after examination

genetics — the study of genes and how characteristics are passed to children

treatment — medical care given to someone who is ill or injured

Homework

Reading, Listening & Writing

Reading

Listening

Writing

Write an essay (140–190 words): 'Is technology making us healthier?'
