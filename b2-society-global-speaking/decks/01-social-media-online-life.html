B2 Exam Speaking Practice

Social Media & Online Life

Practice Part 1 interview, Part 2 photo comparison, Part 3 collaborative task, and extra homework.

Part 1 — Interview

Part 1 — Interview

Answer these questions. Spend about 20 seconds on each.

1. How much time do you spend online each day, and do you think that is too much?
I probably spend three or four hours online, mostly on messaging and social media. I would not say it is excessive, but I do notice it eats into my free time.
2. To what extent has social media changed the way you keep in touch with friends?
It has changed it enormously. I can message friends instantly and see what they are doing, but we meet in person far less often than we used to.
3. Do you find it difficult to switch off from your phone in the evenings?
Yes, it is genuinely difficult. I tell myself I will check one message, and then I end up scrolling for an hour without noticing the time.
4. What kind of content do you tend to share online?
I mostly share photos from trips and the occasional article I find interesting. I rarely post about my private life because I prefer to keep that separate.
5. Has the internet made it easier or harder to tell whether information is reliable?
In some ways it has made it harder. There is so much content that it is increasingly difficult to know which sources are trustworthy and which are simply misleading.
Useful phrases:
To a large extent...I would not say it is...It has changed enormously.I tend to...That's a tricky question because...On balance...
Part 2 — Long Turn

Part 2 — Photo Comparison

Talk for 1 minute. Compare the photos and answer the question.

Why do some people spend so much time on their phones instead of talking to the people around them?
In the first photo, a woman is lying in bed at night, completely absorbed in her phone, with the screen lighting up her face. In the second photo, two people are sitting together in a living room, but instead of talking to each other they are both looking at one phone. I think phones offer constant entertainment and a sense of connection without any effort, which makes them remarkably hard to resist. However, when we use them in company, we can end up feeling strangely isolated even though we are physically together. Personally, I try to put my phone away during meals.
Part 3 — Discussion

Part 3 — Collaborative Task

Discuss with your partner for 2–3 minutes, then decide.

A city wants to help young people use social media more responsibly. Discuss the ideas and decide which two would be most effective.
  • Lessons about online safety in schools
  • Public campaigns showing the risks of screen addiction
  • Free workshops on spotting misinformation
  • More sports and clubs as an alternative to being online
  • Apps that remind users to take regular breaks
A: I think lessons in schools would be the most effective, because young people would learn to question what they see before they form bad habits.
B: That is a fair point, but lessons alone can feel tedious. Free workshops on spotting misinformation might be more practical, since people learn by doing.
A: I agree, and those workshops could also be remarkably useful for parents. What about the reminder apps?
B: They are helpful, but I suspect most teenagers would simply switch them off. I would prefer more sports and clubs, which offer a genuine alternative to being online.
A: So we will choose school lessons and the misinformation workshops.
B: Yes, I am happy with that decision.
Part 4 — Extension

Part 4 — Follow-up Discussion

Answer these deeper questions.

1. How has online communication evolved in the last ten years?
It has evolved remarkably quickly. Text messages have been replaced by short videos and voice notes, and we now expect an immediate reply to almost everything.
2. Do you think social media does more harm than good?
On balance, I think it does more good, because it connects people and gives a voice to those who are usually ignored. The harm comes from how much we use it.
3. Should employers check what job applicants post online?
To some extent they already do, and I can understand why. However, I find it slightly unfair to judge someone for something they posted years ago as a teenager.
4. What responsibilities come with having a large online following?
A large following is a form of influence, so I believe it carries real responsibility. Influencers should be careful about what they promote, especially to young and easily influenced audiences.
Vocabulary

Vocabulary & Phrases

misinformation — false or inaccurate information that is spread, often without anyone intending to deceive

absorbed — so interested in something that you do not notice anything else

scroll through — to move quickly through posts or pages on a screen

influencer — a person with a large online audience who can affect what others buy or think

switch off — to stop thinking about work or technology and relax

Homework

Reading, Listening & Writing

Reading

Read a long article about screen time from a quality news website. Note two arguments the writer makes and whether you find them convincing.

Listening

Listen to a podcast episode about social media and mental health. Write down three phrases the speaker uses to express uncertainty or balance.

Writing

Write an essay (140–190 words): 'Social media has done more to divide people than to bring them together.'
